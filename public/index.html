📖 Environmental Metrics & Indices Plain English Guide
🚶 Pedestrian Exposure LST_norm × (1 - NDVI_norm)
Walking heat stress combining hot unshaded ground with lack of tree canopy. Rated High, Medium, or Low. Formulated in-house from scientific first principles.
🌡️ Ground Heat LST (technical name)
How hot the actual ground surface is in °C — measured from standard 30m × 30m (900 m² pixel) Landsat satellite imagery via Google Earth Engine.
🌿 Green Canopy Score NDVI (technical name)
How much cooling plant cover exists. Derived automatically via Google Earth Engine and verified through physical on-site campus visits.
🏗️ Concrete & Rooftops NDBI (technical name)
Buildings, walkways, and asphalt surfaces that absorb daytime heat and release it at night. Derived automatically in Google Earth Engine.
🌱 Bare Soil Exposure BSI (technical name)
Unplanted dry soil patches baking in direct sunlight — high ground heat with zero evaporative cooling. Computed automatically in GEE.
💧 Moisture Level NDWI (technical name)
How much water is present in plants and ground. Moist ground stays cooler. Derived automatically via Google Earth Engine.
🌳 Tree Cover % Veg% (technical name)
Percentage of ground covered by cooling leaves and shade. Predicted across all 1,323 campus points by our ExtraTreesRegressor ML model.

🛰️ Behind the Scenes

How ShadeRoute Works

Three simple steps — from high-resolution climate observation to your campus plan in seconds.

🛰️ High-Resolution Environmental Monitoring
Landsat 9 and Sentinel-5 earth observation data measure ground surface temperature, atmospheric conditions, tree canopy cover, and built density for every 10-metre sector of the campus and neighbourhood.
🧠 AI Finds the Worst Heat Traps
A machine learning model evaluates which open walkways and plazas are most dangerous — combining heat level, how many students walk there, and whether there's any existing shade — then scores each zone from 0 to 100.
📍 You Decide Where to Cool
Use the sliders to choose how many shade trees or mist sprayers you want to add. ShadeRoute instantly pins the exact optimal locations on the map — so you can see your Campus Action Plan come to life.
🧮 Mathematical Modeling

Scientific Models & Score Formulas

Exact analytical formulas, peer-reviewed literature, and institutional criteria powering every score across ShadeRoute:

🚶 Pedestrian Exposure LST_norm × (1 - NDVI_norm)
Physical outdoor walking heat stress rated into High, Medium, and Low terciles. It uses satellite baseline surface temperature and vegetation context; the result is a planning indicator, not a real-time personal heat exposure measurement.
Formulation & Grounding:
⚠️ Heat Stress Index (HHSI) HI_IMD × [1 + (0.30 × VI)]
Combines official India Meteorological Department (IMD) 5-tier danger thresholds with local demographic vulnerability—giving highest risk amplification to areas with infants (<6 yrs) and elderly citizens (>60 yrs).
Primary Standards:
👥 Vulnerability Index (VI) 45.5% PopRisk + 36.4% FacilityDist + 18.1% CanopyDeficit
Formulated in-house by Team Pipeline Pilots to assess neighborhood vulnerability across the 3 km² study zone. Combines three evidence-based pillars: high-risk age demographics (children <6 and seniors >60), distance to emergency hospitals/schools, and tree shade deficit.
Formulation & Grounding:
🌡️ Feels-Like Heat (HI_IMD) Rothfusz(Air Temp, RH)
Standard apparent temperature formula calculating physiological strain from ambient air temperature and relative humidity (from NASA POWER MERRA-2 satellite data). Categorized into 5 official IMD danger levels.

🎯 Intervention Priority Engine (0–100) 35% VegDeficit + 30% LST + 15% BSI + 10% NDBI + 10% NDWI
Our ExtraTreesRegressor ensemble ML model (train_final_model.py) estimates vegetation fraction across 1,323 SOA campus sample points from satellite-derived indices. The Priority Engine (priority_engine.py) then weights vegetation deficit at 35%, surface-temperature baseline and bare soil, and intersects results with walkways to prioritise candidate tree and misting locations. Cross-validation uncertainty remains material and is not shown as field-measured vegetation.
Primary ML & Spatial Pipeline:
🔥 Multi-Horizon ML Heatwave Prediction Ensemble P(Heatwave | NWP_d+h, Obs_Lag, Climatology) = 0.5·P_RF + 0.5·P_HGB
Operational probabilistic forecasting engine covering same-day nowcast (H0) and 1- to 3-day lead times (H1, H2, H3). Uses a soft-voting ensemble of tuned, class-weighted Random Forest (350 estimators, max depth 8) and Histogram Gradient Boosting (150 estimators, max depth 5). The model consumes 19 biometeorological observation memory lags (7-day trailing temperature, heat index, relative humidity, pressure trends, and radiation) paired with ECMWF/GFS numerical weather predictions and climatological thresholds. Rigorously verified against an out-of-sample temporal holdout (2025–2026) achieving ROC-AUC 0.959–0.974, Accuracy ~95%, and Brier score < 0.04 across all lead horizons.
Primary ML Architecture & Verification:
🌧️ Dual-Metric Precipitation Framework Peak hourly PoP = max(hourly precipitation probability)
Displays the maximum hourly precipitation probability provided by the NWP source and its forecast hour to flag possible convective timing. A daily precipitation probability is not derived by averaging hourly probabilities.
Primary Standards:
🏥 Hospital Patient Surge & Readiness Model Y_t = Y_0 × [1 + (P_hw × β_alert × C_lag × Exposure_mult)]
Epidemiology-informed scenario calculator for emergency readiness, OPD volume, Heat Stroke Unit (HSU) bed needs, and IV fluids at four facilities. It has no local admissions training data and must not be interpreted as a validated patient-count forecast or clinical triage system.
Scope and limits: Facility figures and multipliers are transparent planning assumptions, not measured local demand or bed availability. Guidance documents inform preparedness, but do not validate these numerical projections; facility operators must verify capacity and use official advisories.
